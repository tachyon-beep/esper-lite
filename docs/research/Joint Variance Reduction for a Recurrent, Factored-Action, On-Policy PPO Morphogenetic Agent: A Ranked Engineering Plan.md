# Joint Variance Reduction for a Recurrent, Factored-Action, On-Policy PPO Morphogenetic Agent: A Ranked Engineering Plan

## TL;DR
- **Do Change B first, and do the cheap version of it.** The single highest-value, lowest-risk fix is to replace the single-sample action-conditioned bootstrap/baseline with an **expected/all-action operator** E_op[Q(s,op)] = Σ_op π(op|s)·Q(s,op) (Expected-Sarsa / Expected-Policy-Gradient / Mean-Actor-Critic style). It is unbiased, optimum-preserving, and essentially free because all ops are already evaluated in one batched forward — directly attacking two of your three suspected Problem-B pathologies (mean-zero op advantage and single-sample bootstrap variance).

## Key Findings

**1. The action-dependent-baseline literature predicts your op-head is starved, and that "fixing" it with a fancier action-dependent baseline is a known trap.** Tucker et al. 2018 ("The Mirage of Action-Dependent Baselines in Reinforcement Learning," ICML 2018) decomposed policy-gradient variance and showed that "learned state-action-dependent baselines do not in fact reduce variance over a state-dependent baseline in commonly tested benchmark domains." Critically, they found prior reported gains were implementation artifacts — these methods were "effective not because of unbiased variance reduction, but instead because they trade bias for variance," and that one prior method's reported improvement (in some cases by orders of magnitude) "was caused by a poorly fit value function." This is the core warning: your Q(s,op)-as-baseline is exactly the kind of action-dependent baseline whose benefit is usually illusory, and your own diagnosis (A^op ≈ mean-zero by construction) is the predicted failure mode. The correct, unbiased fix is the *expectation* of the action-dependent quantity, not the single sample.

**2. The expected/all-action operator is the cheapest, highest-impact item and it is well-grounded.** Expected Sarsa (van Seijen et al. 2009, ADPRL), Expected Policy Gradients (Ciosek & Whiteson 2018, AAAI; extended JMLR 2020), and Mean Actor-Critic (Asadi, Allen et al. 2017) all replace a sampled action-value with its expectation under the policy, removing the variance contributed by action sampling at the cost of extra critic evaluations. In your system those evaluations are already paid (all ops evaluated in a batched forward), so E_op[Q(s',op)] for the bootstrap and V(s)=Σ_op π(op|s)Q(s,op) for the baseline are nearly free. This converts your single-sample bootstrap (which "injects policy-dependent variance into the value target," per your own diagnosis) into a lower-variance expectation.

**3. The marginal/expected V(s) baseline is the correct control variate and it is what COMA reduces to per-agent — but the full COMA machinery is empirically disappointing.** COMA (Foerster et al. 2018, AAAI) marginalizes one agent's action while holding others fixed; its baseline is provably unbiased ("the expected contribution of the counterfactual baseline to the policy gradient is zero"). But the empirical record is poor: the StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge paper (Samvelyan et al. 2019, AAMAS) reports that "IQL, VDN, and QMIX all significantly outperform COMA, demonstrating the sample efficiency of off-policy value-based methods over on-policy policy gradient methods" — COMA is beaten even by independent Q-learning. The EPyMARL benchmark (Papoudakis et al. 2021, NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks) finds COMA among the weakest CTDE methods. Theory explains why: Kuba et al. 2021 (NeurIPS, "Settling the Variance of Multi-Agent Policy Gradients," pp. 13458–13470) derive the variance-optimal baseline and prove (Theorem 2) that "COMA baseline essentially allows to drop the number of agents from the order of excess variance" — it cancels cross-agent noise but **not** the agent's own-exploration variance, so it is unbiased but variance-*suboptimal*. Zhou et al. 2020 (LICA, NeurIPS) note the counterfactual advantage → 0 in expectation under a perfect critic. Verdict: marginalizing per-head buys little over a single marginal V(s) for your shared-scalar-advantage structure, and adds coupling risk.

**4. Reward decomposition (Hybrid Reward Architecture) is the principled home for the high-CoV counterfactual term.** van Seijen et al. 2017 (NeurIPS, "Hybrid Reward Architecture") learns a separate value function per reward component: "Because each component typically only depends on a subset of all features, the corresponding value function can be approximated more easily by a low-dimensional representation, enabling more effective learning," achieving above-human play on Ms. Pac-Man. Notably, HRA's heads are trained with the Expected Sarsa update — reinforcing the link to Finding 2. RD2 and distributional reward decomposition (Lin et al. 2019, NeurIPS) extend this. This is the right framing for Change A: rather than regress one critic on PBRS + terminal + rent + (CoV≈1.9 counterfactual), split off the high-variance counterfactual term into its own head so it stops polluting the main value target's EV.

**5. PBRS keeps the optimum but changes the function the critic must learn — and clipping breaks the guarantee.** Ng, Harada & Russell 1999 (ICML) proved potential-based shaping F=γΦ(s')−Φ(s) preserves the optimal policy set. Crucially, under PBRS the critic learns V_shaped = V_orig − Φ (Wiewiora 2003 showed PBRS is equivalent to Q-value initialization). Your seed-lifecycle potential table is optimum-safe in isolation — but two caveats bite: (i) your **escrow delta-clip of 2.0 breaks telescoping when it binds, which voids the Ng et al. invariance**; and (ii) dynamic potentials are only safe under the Devlin & Kudenko 2012 (AAMAS) time-indexed extension. Harutyunyan et al. 2015 (AAAI) shows an *arbitrary* reward (like your counterfactual signal) can be converted into a policy-invariant dynamic *advice potential* learned as an auxiliary value function — the rigorous bridge if you want to keep the counterfactual signal as shaping without biasing the optimum.

**6. Critic-free group baselines (GRPO/RLOO) are documented to fail on exactly your task profile.** GRPO (Shao et al. 2024, DeepSeekMath) and RLOO (Ahmadian et al. 2024, ACL "Back to Basics") drop the learned critic for a group/leave-one-out baseline. But the first systematic classical-RL study, de Oliveira et al., "Learning Without Critics? Revisiting GRPO in Classical RL Environments" (arXiv 2511.03527, Nov 2025), finds that "learned critics remain essential for long-horizon tasks — all critic-free baselines underperform PPO except in short-horizon environments like CartPole where episodic returns can be effective," with "the presence of early termination" being the critical enabler. Your task is long-horizon, dense-reward, non-terminating per-step — the regime where these methods underperform PPO. They would also remove the bootstrap you rely on under TBPTT truncation.

## Details

### Track A — reward / value-target variance

**A1 — Control variates from a hand-built signal (the counterfactual term).** The textbook-correct way to use a high-variance, roughly-unbiased per-step signal for variance reduction is to subtract its *conditional expectation* so it integrates to zero (a control variate), not to add it to the reward. Stein-style action-dependent control variates (Liu et al. 2018, ICLR, "Action-dependent Control Variates for Policy Optimization via Stein's Identity") and Q-Prop (Gu et al. 2017, ICLR) are the canonical constructions. **But Tucker et al. 2018 (ICML) is the decisive critique**: in benchmark domains these did not actually reduce variance beyond a state-dependent baseline, and apparent gains were implementation artifacts (a poorly-fit value function). **Implication:** using the counterfactual signal as a *policy-gradient* control variate is high-risk/low-evidence. Using it via *reward decomposition* (A3) or *as a separate prediction target* is the safer route. Confidence: high that the naive control-variate route is a trap (strong empirical evidence from Tucker et al.).

**A2 — Counterfactual information as a baseline, not a reward.** Mesnard et al. 2021 (ICML, "Counterfactual Credit Assignment in Model-Free RL") condition value functions on *future* trajectory information to build future-conditional baselines/critics that are "provably low variance," while constraining the hindsight features to exclude the agent's own action to avoid bias. This is the principled home for a leave-one-out contribution signal: feed it as input to a *baseline/critic*, not into the return. Caveat: their low-variance claims are largely theory plus illustrative tasks, not large-scale benchmarks — treat as theory-leaning. Confidence: medium that this directly helps EV stability; high that it is the conceptually correct place for counterfactual info.

**A3 — Reward decomposition / multi-stream critics (RECOMMENDED for Change A).** Hybrid Reward Architecture (van Seijen et al. 2017, NeurIPS) is the strongest fit: give the high-CoV counterfactual component its own value head, regress the main head on the low-variance remainder (PBRS deltas + terminal + rent). Because the main-head target loses the CoV≈1.9–2.7 term, its EV should rise and stabilize. Compatible with your PopArt-lite normalizer (apply per-head). Confidence: medium-high; HRA has strong empirical wins (Ms. Pac-Man) but on value-based, Expected-Sarsa-trained agents, not PPO-GAE — flag as a transfer with limited direct PPO evidence.

**A4 — PBRS and the learnability of the value target.** Under PBRS the critic learns V_orig − Φ. If Φ is well-chosen, this can *reduce* the magnitude/variance of the regression target and help EV; if Φ is noisy or dynamic, it can hurt. Your discrete seed-lifecycle potential is smooth and bounded, which is favorable. Devlin & Kudenko 2012 (AAMAS) licenses time-varying potentials; Harutyunyan et al. 2015 (AAAI) licenses turning arbitrary rewards into potentials via an auxiliary learned value function. Confidence: high on optimum-preservation theory; medium on the EV effect (depends on Φ quality).

**A5 — Target-variance reduction in PPO specifically.** PopArt (van Hasselt et al. 2016, NeurIPS) adaptively normalizes value targets while preserving outputs; you run a PopArt-lite variant. Note your variant does NOT rescale the final layer, so it normalizes target scale but not the representation — a known partial implementation. For recurrence, R2D2 (Kapturowski et al. 2019, ICLR) is the key reference: stored-state staleness and representational drift are real, and **burn-in** + stored-state initialization materially improve recurrent value learning. Your "anchored reference pass" (unroll ratios from t=0 hidden state) is a sensible analog, but confirm value targets are computed from on-policy-recomputed hidden states, not stale stored states, to avoid the drift R2D2 documents. Confidence: high.

**A6 — Escrow / telescoping and observation augmentation.** Your escrow delta-clip of 2.0 **breaks the PBRS telescoping property when it binds**, forfeiting the Ng et al. 1999 optimum-invariance guarantee — a genuine silent-bias risk. The cleaner alternative is to **put the prior potential state (and the unrealized-credit target) into the observation** so the recurrent critic can predict the delta itself rather than receiving a clipped shaped reward. Putting Markovian-completion/shaping state into the observation is a recognized technique (it restores Markovianity for the value function, consistent with Wiewiora's potential≡Q-init equivalence). Pitfalls: it enlarges the observation and can let the policy overfit to the potential signal; and it relocates the learning problem into the critic rather than reducing reward variance. Confidence: medium; reasoned from theory rather than a single benchmark.

### Track B — action-conditioned baseline / critic redesign

**B1 — Action-independent vs action-dependent baselines.** Any baseline b(s) independent of the sampled action is unbiased. An action-dependent baseline b(s,a) is unbiased only if you correct for it (Q-Prop's correction term) or take its expectation; otherwise it biases the gradient. Your Q(s,op_taken) used as the op-head *baseline* is action-dependent and uncorrected — the textbook bias/variance hazard, and Tucker et al. 2018 says the upside is usually illusory anyway. Verdict: Q(s,op) as the op-head baseline plausibly *hurts* (partial advantage cancellation + bias) rather than helps. Confidence: high.

**B2 — Expected / all-action bootstrapping (THE cheapest, highest-impact item).** Replace single-sample Q(s',op'~β) with E_op[Q(s',op)] = Σ_op π(op|s')Q(s',op). Grounded in Expected Sarsa (van Seijen et al. 2009), Expected Policy Gradients (Ciosek & Whiteson 2018, AAAI), Mean Actor-Critic (Allen et al. 2017). Normally the tradeoff is "extra critic evaluations vs. variance reduction," but you already evaluate all ops in one batched forward, so the expectation is essentially free. Removes the policy-dependent sampling variance from the bootstrap target; unbiased/optimum-preserving. Confidence: high that it reduces target variance; this is the flagship recommendation.

**B3 — Per-head counterfactual baselines (KEY question; verdict: NO as primary fix).** Structurally your causally-masked heads are a cooperative multi-agent problem with a shared scalar advantage, and COMA (Foerster et al. 2018, AAAI) is the exact generalization: marginalize each head's action holding others fixed. It is unbiased. **But the empirical and theoretical record argues against it as your primary tool:** Samvelyan et al. 2019 (SMAC, AAMAS) report "IQL, VDN, and QMIX all significantly outperform COMA"; Papoudakis et al. 2021 (EPyMARL, NeurIPS D&B) rank it among the weakest CTDE methods; Kuba et al. 2021 (NeurIPS) prove its baseline is unbiased but variance-suboptimal (cancels cross-agent noise but not own-exploration variance); Zhou et al. 2020 (LICA, NeurIPS) show the counterfactual advantage → 0 under a perfect critic. For a *single* shared-advantage op head, per-head marginalization collapses to the marginal V(s) baseline of B4 anyway. There is a recent precedent applying the COMA baseline to factored *single-pipeline* decision heads (MA²ML, Wang et al. 2023, CVPR) with reported gains, and 2026 LLM preprints (CCPO, C3) extend it to sequential heads — but these are thin/recent and not broadly reproduced. Verdict: implement the marginal V(s) (B4), not full per-head counterfactuals; keep per-head counterfactuals as an optional experiment only if B4+B2 plateau. Confidence: medium-high.

**B4 — Marginal V(s)=Σ_op π(op|s)·Q(s,op) as baseline (RECOMMENDED for Change B).** The correct control variate: the op term cancels in expectation, making it an unbiased action-independent baseline, while reusing your existing Q(s,op) head and the already-paid all-ops forward. It is exactly COMA's per-agent construction specialized to one shared head, without the multi-head coupling. Pitfalls: (i) it couples to a moving policy π(op|s), so compute it with the *anchored reference* policy used for the ratio to stay consistent across the K inner epochs; (ii) watch the off-by-one between the baseline at s and the bootstrap at s' — both should use the expected operator (B2) for consistency; (iii) under PopArt-lite, compute the marginalization in normalized value space and de-normalize once. Alternative: learn a separate V(s) head directly (simpler gradient, but adds parameters and a second regression target). Recommendation: start with the marginal Σπ·Q (no new parameters), add a dedicated V(s) head only if the marginal estimate is too coupled/noisy. Confidence: high on correctness; medium on which of marginal-vs-separate-head wins empirically.

**B5 — Keep Q(s,op) as an auxiliary/telemetry head.** Dueling networks (Wang et al. 2016, ICML) show V and A can be decomposed within one network with an identifiability constraint (subtract the mean/argmax advantage). You can expose Q(s,op) and A(s,op)=Q(s,op)−V(s) for credit telemetry while using V(s) as the actual baseline. Risk: a shared trunk means the auxiliary Q-head's gradients can perturb the primary V(s) representation; mitigate with stop-gradient from the aux head into the trunk, or a small loss weight. Confidence: medium-high.

**B6 — Factored-action PG hygiene.** Action branching (Tavakoli et al. 2018, AAAI) establishes the shared-trunk + per-branch head architecture you use and the importance of the shared module for coordination. On normalization: your current global advantage standardization is "dominated by the always-active op head," under-scaling the sparse sub-heads (active ~18–22% of steps). **Turn ON per-head re-standardization** (mean/std over the *causally-active* steps per head), because a global std computed mostly from op-head steps mis-scales sub-head advantages. This interacts cleanly with B4 and is low-risk. Caveat: per-head standardization slightly changes effective per-head learning rates — retune entropy/clip if needed. Confidence: medium-high.

### Track C — synthesis

**C1 — Complementary or substitutes? VERDICT: complementary, with B first.** B (expected operator + marginal V baseline) repairs the *estimator*: it removes single-sample bootstrap variance and gives the op head a non-degenerate learning signal. A (de-shape via reward decomposition) repairs the *target*: it removes the CoV≈1.9–2.7 counterfactual term from the main regressand. Fixing B does **not** subsume A — even a perfect expected baseline still regresses on a target containing the high-variance counterfactual reward, so EV will remain volatile because the target itself is volatile. Conversely, A alone leaves the degenerate mean-zero op advantage and single-sample bootstrap untouched. **Order: B before A**, because B is nearly free, unambiguously unbiased, and you should measure how much EV-volatility B removes before taking on the larger engineering and A/B-testing cost of reward decomposition.

**C2 — Critic-free (GRPO/RLOO)? VERDICT: does not fit; do not adopt.** It would dissolve both problems on paper (no value target to de-noise, no action baseline to fix), but: (i) the only systematic classical-RL evidence (de Oliveira et al. 2025, arXiv 2511.03527) shows critic-free baselines underperform PPO on long-horizon, non-terminating tasks and work mainly with early termination and short horizons — the opposite of your profile; (ii) you would lose the bootstrap V(s') needed for credit assignment under TBPTT truncation (group baselines assume whole-episode rollouts and return comparison); (iii) paired-seed/group designs require multiple comparable rollouts from the same state, which your expensive host-network training does not cheaply provide. GRPO/RLOO pay off at large batch / many-samples-per-prompt regimes (RLOO papers use k≈10–16, batch 128); your per-rollout cost makes that uneconomical. Confidence: high.

**C3 — Recommended ranked plan.** See Recommendations.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (do now; cheapest, highest-confidence, unbiased).**
1. **Expected/all-action bootstrap + marginal V(s) baseline (B2 + B4).** Replace the single behaviour-sampled bootstrap Q(s',op'~β) with E_op[Q(s',op)]=Σπ(op|s')Q(s',op), and replace the op-head baseline Q(s,op_taken) with V(s)=Σπ(op|s)Q(s,op). Compute both in normalized value space using the *anchored reference* policy. Cost: low (all ops already in the batched forward). Expected effect: removes single-sample bootstrap variance and un-starves the op head; EV level and stability both improve. Papers: van Seijen 2009; Ciosek & Whiteson 2018; Allen et al. 2017; Foerster et al. 2018 (B4 = COMA specialized to one head). **Gate:** measure EV median and EV inter-decile range over training. If EV-volatility drops materially and median rises, proceed; if not, audit the PopArt-lite normalization path and stored-state freshness (A5/R2D2) before anything else.
2. **Turn on per-head advantage re-standardization (B6)** over causally-active steps per head. Cost: trivial. Effect: corrects sub-head under-scaling caused by op-head-dominated global std. **Gate:** monitor per-head gradient norms and sub-head entropy; retune entropy coef if sub-heads collapse.
3. **Keep Q(s,op) as a stop-gradient telemetry head (B5).** Cost: trivial. Retains credit diagnostics without destabilizing V(s).

**Stage 2 (do if EV is still volatile after Stage 1; medium cost, optimum-safe if careful).**
4. **De-shape via Hybrid-Reward-Architecture reward decomposition (A3 + A2).** Move the high-CoV counterfactual term OUT of the main regressand into its own value head (or a future-conditional auxiliary critic à la Mesnard et al. 2021), and regress the main critic on PBRS deltas + terminal accuracy + rent. Use your existing reward-mode A/B harness (shaped/escrow/basic/...) to compare main-head EV with vs. without the counterfactual term in the target. Cost: medium. Effect: main-head EV rises and stabilizes because its target loses the CoV≈1.9–2.7 component. Papers: van Seijen et al. 2017; Lin et al. 2019; Mesnard et al. 2021. **Gate:** main-head EV stability improves AND policy performance (val accuracy net of params) does not regress. If performance regresses, the counterfactual signal was carrying real credit — reintroduce it via Harutyunyan et al. 2015 dynamic advice potential (policy-invariant) rather than as raw reward.
5. **Fix the escrow telescoping break (A6).** Either remove the delta-clip (restoring Ng et al. 1999 invariance) or, preferably, move the prior potential / unrealized-credit state into the observation and let the recurrent critic predict the delta. **Gate:** verify the telescoping unit test passes unclipped; confirm no optimum drift via a held-out reward-mode comparison.

**Stage 3 (only if Stages 1–2 plateau; experimental, thinner evidence).**
6. **Per-head counterfactual baselines (B3, COMA-style)** for the sparse sub-heads, marginalizing each head's action while holding others fixed. Treat as an experiment, not a default — the benchmark record (Samvelyan 2019; Kuba 2021) says the marginal benefit over a good state baseline is small and variance-suboptimal. Use the MA²ML (CVPR 2023) factored-pipeline precedent as the implementation template.

**Explicitly do NOT:** switch to GRPO/RLOO/off-policy (C2); use the counterfactual signal as a raw policy-gradient control variate (A1/Tucker trap); leave the escrow clip in place while claiming optimum-invariance.

**Benchmarks that would change the plan:** If Stage-1 EV remains negative/volatile despite a correct expected operator, the problem is more likely critic capacity / recurrent state staleness (R2D2 regime) than baseline design — pivot to burn-in and stored-state fixes. If de-shaping (Stage 2) regresses task performance, the counterfactual term is load-bearing credit and must be retained as a policy-invariant potential.

## Caveats and Confidence

- **Strong empirical evidence (high confidence):** Tucker et al. 2018 (action-dependent baselines are usually a mirage; gains "trade bias for variance"); COMA's empirical underperformance vs. QMIX/VDN/IQL (Samvelyan 2019, AAMAS; Papoudakis 2021, NeurIPS D&B); critic-free methods underperform on long-horizon/non-terminating tasks (de Oliveira et al. 2025); R2D2 recurrent state-staleness effects (Kapturowski 2019); PBRS optimum-invariance (Ng et al. 1999, formally proven); Kuba et al. 2021 variance decomposition (NeurIPS, peer-reviewed).
- **Theory-leaning / thinner evidence (medium confidence):** Mesnard et al. 2021 low-variance claims (illustrative tasks, not large benchmarks); HRA transferring from value-based, Expected-Sarsa-trained Atari agents to PPO-GAE (no direct large-scale PPO study located); the EV effect of de-shaping (reasoned, not measured in your exact setting); whether Kuba et al.'s optimal baseline is practically worth its complexity.
- **Contested / unsettled (flag, do not imply consensus):** whether marginal Σπ·Q vs. a separately-learned V(s) head wins in practice — both are defensible and no head-to-head exists for a factored single-agent PPO critic; whether per-head counterfactual baselines help factored *single-agent* settings (the only supporting evidence — MA²ML CVPR 2023, and 2026 LLM preprints CCPO/C3 — is recent and not yet broadly reproduced); whether putting shaping state in the observation reliably beats clipped shaping (reasoned from Wiewiora 2003 equivalence, not benchmarked in PPO).
- **Bias traps specific to your system:** (1) the escrow delta-clip silently breaks PBRS invariance — a real optimum-changing bias hiding in "shaping." (2) An uncorrected action-dependent baseline Q(s,op_taken) biases the op-head gradient; only its *expectation* is safe. (3) Computing the marginal baseline with the *current* (not anchored-reference) policy across the K inner epochs reintroduces policy-dependent variance and a subtle bias — pin it to the reference pass. (4) Per-head re-standardization changes effective per-head step sizes; retune entropy/clip. (5) Under PopArt-lite, do the Σπ·Q marginalization in normalized space and de-normalize once, or you will double-count the normalizer.
