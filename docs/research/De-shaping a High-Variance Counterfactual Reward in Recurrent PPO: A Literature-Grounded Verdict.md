# De-shaping a High-Variance Counterfactual Reward in Recurrent PPO: A Literature-Grounded Verdict

## TL;DR
- **The central hypothesis is sound and supported by theory and practice: move the high-CoV counterfactual-contribution term OUT of the return the critic regresses on, keep the low-variance PBRS + sparse-terminal + rent return as the regressand, and reintroduce the counterfactual signal as a state-only baseline / auxiliary prediction target — NOT as a state-action control variate you expect to cut gradient variance, because Tucker et al. (2018) showed "learned state-action-dependent baselines do not in fact reduce variance over a state-dependent baseline in commonly tested benchmark domains."**
- **De-shaping is COMPLEMENTARY to, not a substitute for, fixing the action-conditioned Q(s,op) critic.** They attack two different variance sources: de-shaping lowers *value-target* variance (helps EV directly); the Q→marginal-V fix removes *policy-dependent baseline/bootstrap* variance that Tucker (2018), van Seijen et al. (2009, Expected Sarsa) and Allen/Asadi et al. (2017, Mean Actor-Critic) all identify as avoidable. Do both; the critic fix is the higher-leverage one.
- **Biggest trap:** your escrow mode's delta-clip and any *learned* potential break the Ng et al. (1999) telescoping guarantee and can silently move the optimum. Keep the de-shaped potential strictly state-(history-)based and unclipped (use Devlin & Kudenko 2012 dynamic PBRS or Forbes et al. 2024 episodic-boundary form if you need time/history dependence), and put the prior potential state in the observation so the recurrent critic predicts deltas cleanly.

## Key Findings

1. **Use the counterfactual term as a state-dependent baseline / auxiliary target, not as an action-dependent control variate.** The "control variate" framing is exactly what *The Mirage of Action-Dependent Baselines* (Tucker, Bhupatiraju, Gu, Turner, Ghahramani, Levine; ICML 2018, arXiv:1802.10031) debunks: learned state-action-dependent baselines "do not in fact reduce variance over a state-dependent baseline in commonly tested benchmark domains," and reported gains in Q-Prop/Stein/LAX traced to bias-introducing implementation details that "trade variance for bias." Their recommended fix is a better-fit, horizon-aware *state* value function. So the variance win comes from de-shaping the regressand and fitting V(s) well — not from a Stein/Q-Prop-style action-dependent CV.

2. **Counterfactual credit-as-baseline has a principled home.** Counterfactual Credit Assignment (Mesnard et al., ICML 2021) and Hindsight Credit Assignment (Harutyunyan et al., NeurIPS 2019) use future-/outcome-conditional value functions as *baselines/critics* that are "provably low variance" and kept unbiased by constraining hindsight info to exclude the agent's own action. COCOA (Meulemans et al., NeurIPS 2023 spotlight, arXiv:2306.16803) shows measuring contributions w.r.t. *rewards/rewarding-object representations* (not states) avoids the spurious-credit failure that collapses HCA to REINFORCE. Your leave-one-out contribution is a reward-referenced counterfactual — closer to the COCOA-correct variant than to state-HCA.

3. **Separate critic head for the high-CoV component is well-grounded (Hybrid Reward Architecture).** HRA (van Seijen, Fatemi, Romoff, Laroche, Barnes, Tsang; NeurIPS 2017, arXiv:1706.04208) learns a separate value function per reward component, each easier to approximate; it was demonstrated on a fruit-collection toy problem and on Atari Ms. Pac-Man, where it "not only surpass[ed] the human high-score of 266,330 points, [but achieved] the maximum possible score of 999,990 points in less than 3,000 episodes." RD2 (Lin et al., 2020) and distributional multi-dimensional-reward critics (2021) extend it. A dedicated head that regresses the counterfactual stream while the main head regresses the clean return isolates the noisy target and should stabilize the main head's EV.

4. **PBRS changes WHAT the critic must learn, and de-shaping helps learnability.** Under PBRS the learned value becomes V_shaped = V_orig − Φ (Wiewiora's Q-init equivalence). If Φ tracks true value, targets shrink and become easier; if Φ is noisy/learned, it adds target variance. Ng et al. (1999) guarantees optimum-invariance only for static, state-only Φ; Devlin & Kudenko (2012, AAMAS) extends to time-varying Φ(s,t); Harutyunyan et al. (2015, AAAI) shows arbitrary reward functions can be folded into a *learned secondary potential* (DPBA) preserving the optimum in expectation; Forbes et al. (2024, PBIM/GRM, arXiv:2410.12197) extends optimality-preservation to complex non-Markovian shaping under an episodic boundary condition.

5. **PPO-specific target-variance practice favors de-shaping + good normalization.** PopArt (van Hasselt et al., NeurIPS 2016; Hessel et al., AAAI 2019) adaptively normalizes value targets AND rescales the head's final layer — your "PopArt-lite" omits the rescaling, so it controls scale but not the gradient-magnitude pathology PopArt was built for. Andrychowicz et al. (ICLR 2021, "What Matters for On-Policy RL") give the verbatim recommendation to "use GAE with λ = 0.9 but neither Huber loss nor PPO-style value loss clipping," finding "the Huber loss performed worse than MSE in all environments." For recurrence, R2D2 (Kapturowski, Ostrovski, Quan, Munos, Dabney; ICLR 2019) shows stored-state staleness/representational drift materially distort value learning — recompute hidden states and use burn-in.

6. **Group-relative / critic-free baselines (GRPO, RLOO) are a cleaner cut ONLY if you can afford paired rollouts.** RLOO (Ahmadian et al., ACL 2024) and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) drop the learned baseline entirely, using a within-group/leave-one-out mean as the baseline — which sidesteps your Q(s,op) pathology completely. RLOO "outperforms a highly-tuned PPO variant at 6.9B all the while training up to 3x faster and using 70% less RAM" (with +10.4% win-rate on TL;DR and +14.5% on HH over PPO using four samples per prompt). But they assume multiple comparable rollouts per "prompt" (here: per host/seed-config), give a single trajectory-level advantage (weak per-step credit), and Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., COLM 2025, arXiv:2503.20783) showed GRPO's per-response length and reward-std normalization introduce optimization bias and removes both. This is a real alternative but it trades your per-step credit-assignment signal for batch-level comparison.

7. **"Put the shaping state in the observation" is recognized and preferable to clipping the potential delta.** Clipping a potential difference (your escrow delta-clip of 2.0) breaks telescoping and voids the Ng et al. guarantee when it binds. Feeding Φ(s) (or the lifecycle-stage / unrealized-credit state) into the observation lets the recurrent critic predict γΦ(s′)−Φ(s) as part of its target without clipping; this is the Markovian-augmentation trick used to make non-Markovian shaping admissible (consistent with Forbes et al. 2024's history-dependent potentials).

## Details

### RQ1 — Control variates from a hand-built signal: theory vs. the "mirage"
A control variate subtracts a term with known/estimable conditional expectation so it leaves the gradient *unbiased* while (ideally) reducing variance. For policy gradients, a baseline b(s) subtracted from the return is the canonical zero-bias CV because E_a[∇log π(a|s) b(s)] = 0. Extending to b(s,a) (action-dependent) keeps zero bias only if you correct with E_a[b(s,a)] (Q-Prop, Gu et al., ICLR 2017; Stein CVs, Liu et al., ICLR 2018, arXiv:1710.11198; factorized baselines, Wu et al., ICLR 2018). **Tucker et al. (2018, ICML)** decomposed the gradient variance into Σ_τ (trajectory) + Σ_a (action) + Σ_s (state) and showed only Σ_a is touched by an action-dependent baseline, and that on MuJoCo/LQG Σ_τ dominates — so action dependence buys little, and prior "wins" came from bias-trading implementation bugs. Their fix: a horizon-aware V(s) parameterization that "improves performance compared with the state-action-dependent baseline without biasing the underlying method." Follow-ups: Trajectory-wise CVs (2019) and Coordinate-wise CVs (2021) refine the idea but remain niche. **Implication for you:** treat the counterfactual term as informing a *state* baseline / auxiliary target, and do NOT expect a Stein/Q-Prop-style action-conditioned CV to reduce variance — that's the mirage. (Tucker's result is empirical on MuJoCo/LQG, not a universal theorem; they note action-dependent baselines DO help when a single action strongly swings the return, e.g. Cliffworld — but your variance is dominated by a dense reward term, not single-action swings.)

### RQ2 — Counterfactual/future-conditional baselines
- **CCA (Mesnard et al., ICML 2021, PMLR 139:7654–7664):** future-conditional value functions as baselines/critics, provably lower variance, unbiased by excluding action information from the hindsight features. Validated on illustrative + challenging tasks (DeepMind-scale, not toy-only).
- **HCA (Harutyunyan et al., NeurIPS 2019):** reweights returns by a hindsight distribution ratio h(a|s,outcome)/π(a|s); unbiased in theory, but degrades to high-variance REINFORCE when contributions are measured w.r.t. states.
- **COCOA (Meulemans, Schug, Kobayashi, Daw, Wayne; NeurIPS 2023):** measure contributions w.r.t. rewards/representations of rewarding objects → lower-variance estimates; the correct successor for a *reward-referenced* leave-one-out signal like yours.
These are the principled home for your contribution signal: as a baseline/critic input, not a reward addend.

### RQ3 — Reward decomposition / multi-stream critics
HRA (van Seijen et al., NeurIPS 2017) — separate value per component; achieved the maximum 999,990-point score on Ms. Pac-Man. RD2 (Lin et al., 2020) and distributional multi-dimensional reward critics (2021) are close successors. UNREAL (Jaderberg et al., ICLR 2017) is the auxiliary-task analog: extra value/reward-prediction heads on a shared CNN-LSTM improve representation and stability — directly relevant to your shared LSTM trunk. **Verdict:** a dedicated head for the high-CoV counterfactual stream (either a value head HRA-style or an auxiliary regression target UNREAL-style) is the lowest-risk way to retain the signal's information while keeping it out of the main regressand.

### RQ4 — PBRS and the value function
Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) prove F(s,s′)=γΦ(s′)−Φ(s) preserves the optimal policy and (Wiewiora 2003) is equivalent to initializing Q with Φ; the learned object becomes V−Φ. Devlin & Kudenko (2012) extend to dynamic Φ(s,t) (preserves optimum and Nash equilibria). Harutyunyan et al. (2015) express arbitrary reward functions as a learned secondary potential (DPBA), capturing the input reward in expectation — the literature's most direct license for "keep a signal as shaping without biasing the optimum." Forbes et al. (2024, PBIM/GRM) generalize optimality-preservation to complex, non-Markovian/trainable shaping under an episodic boundary condition; their action-dependent extension (Forbes et al., 2025, ADOPS, arXiv:2505.12611) covers sequence-dependent pseudo-rewards. Note PIES (Behboudian et al.) flags a flaw in naive DPBA and offers a corrected explicit-shaping schedule that decays shaping to zero — relevant if you ever let the potential be learned.

### RQ5 — Return/target variance reduction in PPO
PopArt (van Hasselt et al. 2016; Hessel et al. 2019) is the reference for value-target normalization; critically it rescales the output layer to preserve outputs as statistics shift — your PopArt-lite omits this, so consider full PopArt if value-target scale drifts as the policy improves. Return-based scaling (Schaul et al., 2021) is a lighter successor. Andrychowicz et al. (ICLR 2021) recommend GAE (λ≈0.9), MSE (not Huber), and no PPO value-loss clipping. Dense vs sparse: dense rewards generally aid critic learnability but a dense *high-variance* term does the opposite — it inflates target variance and depresses EV, which is exactly your diagnosis. Recurrence: R2D2 (Kapturowski et al., ICLR 2019) stored-state vs recomputed-state discrepancy and burn-in are the canonical fixes; your "anchored reference pass" recomputing from t=0 hidden state is aligned with R2D2's stored-state remedy.

### RQ6 — Critic-free group-relative baselines
GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024) replace the learned baseline with a group/leave-one-out mean over G rollouts per prompt; RLOO is unbiased, GRPO's std-normalization is biased (Dr. GRPO, Liu et al., 2025, removes it, reaching 43.3% on AIME 2024 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B vs SimpleRL-Zero-7B's 36.0%). RLOO outperformed a tuned PPO at 6.9B with ~70% less memory and up to 3× faster — but in the LLM setting with cheap parallel rollouts and outcome-level rewards. For your morphogenetic setup, a paired-seed / paired-host design (same host, different growth interventions, shared seed) could yield a clean group baseline that entirely sidesteps the Q(s,op) problem — but it gives trajectory-level advantages (weak per-step credit) and needs enough comparable rollouts per group to beat a learned V(s). **Verdict:** a complementary option to prototype, not a drop-in replacement, and it does NOT by itself preserve the per-step counterfactual credit you value.

### RQ7 — Escrow/telescoping and observation augmentation
Clipping γΦ(s′)−Φ(s) breaks the telescoping sum, so the path-integral cancellation that gives Ng-invariance fails whenever the clip binds — the optimum can shift. The recognized alternative is to make the shaping state part of the observation so the (recurrent) critic learns to predict the delta as part of its value target rather than receiving a clipped reward addend; this is the standard Markovian-augmentation move that makes non-Markovian/history-dependent shaping admissible (Forbes et al. 2024 history-potentials; consistent with Devlin & Kudenko 2012 dynamic potentials). Pitfalls: (a) augmenting the observation enlarges the state and can slow critic fitting; (b) if the potential is *learned* and fed as input, you risk a feedback loop and must decay it (PIES) or hold it fixed; (c) you lose the explicit policy-invariance guarantee unless the augmented potential still satisfies a telescoping/boundary condition.

### The action-conditioned Q(s,op) critic — complementary fix, higher leverage
Your value head estimates Q(s,op) and uses ONE behaviour-sampled Q(s′,op′~β) as the GAE bootstrap. Three independent results say this injects avoidable, policy-dependent variance into the target:
- **van Seijen, van Hasselt, Whiteson & Wiering (2009, IEEE ADPRL), Expected Sarsa:** replacing the single sampled next-action value with the expectation Σ_a π(a|s′)Q(s′,a) yields lower-variance updates — "Expected Sarsa's updates have zero variance" in deterministic environments, enabling a learning rate of 1.
- **Allen, Asadi, Roderick, Mohamed, Konidaris, Littman (2017), Mean Actor-Critic:** the sampled TD-error "has higher variance (due to the environmental dynamics) than methods that explicitly compute Q(s,a)−V(s)," and V is recoverable for free as V(s)=Σ_a π(a|s)Q(s,a).
- **Ciosek & Whiteson (2018, AAAI), Expected Policy Gradients; Petit et al. 2019, All-Action PG; Nauman & Cygan (ICML 2023), Many-Actions PG:** integrating/marginalizing over actions provably reduces gradient variance vs single-action sampling — though substituting a *learned* Q to do the marginalization re-introduces bias (Nauman & Cygan; and the bias-trading Tucker 2018 diagnosed in Q-Prop's code).

So the correct cheap fix is to bootstrap and baseline with the **marginal V(s)=Σ_op π(op|s)·Q(s,op)** (you already have all per-op Q values; the op head is discrete and small — this is exactly the Mean Actor-Critic / Expected-Sarsa marginalization, essentially free) rather than a single sampled op. This is **complementary** to de-shaping: de-shaping shrinks the *regression target's* intrinsic variance; marginalization removes the *baseline/bootstrap sampling* variance. Per Tucker (2018), the marginal/horizon-aware V is also where the real variance reduction lives — so prioritize it.

## Recommendations (staged)

**Stage 0 — Instrument before changing (~1 day).** Log, per reward component, the variance contribution to the return and the per-head EV. Confirm component #1 (counterfactual, CoV ~1.9/2.7) dominates target variance. Benchmark to beat: current median EV ~0.31, volatile. Threshold to advance: counterfactual term contributes >40% of return variance.

**Stage 1 — Marginalize the critic baseline/bootstrap (highest leverage, lowest risk; ~2 days).** Replace single-sampled Q(s′,op′~β) with V(s)=Σ_op π(op|s)·Q(s,op) for BOTH the GAE baseline and bootstrap (Mean Actor-Critic / Expected Sarsa). Keep Q(s,op) head if you want per-op diagnostics but compute advantages off the marginal V. Expected effect: directly lower value-target variance and stabilize EV. This is the change Tucker (2018), van Seijen (2009) and Allen/Asadi (2017) all point to. Benchmark: EV variance (std over training) should drop; median EV should rise. If EV does not stabilize, the residual is target noise → proceed to Stage 2.

**Stage 2 — De-shape the regressand (the central hypothesis; ~3 days, cheap given your reward-mode variants).** A/B "shaped" vs a new "deshaped" mode where the critic regresses on return = PBRS deltas + sparse terminal accuracy + rent only. Reintroduce the counterfactual term via EITHER (2a) a separate HRA-style value head, OR (2b) a UNREAL-style auxiliary prediction target on the shared LSTM, OR (2c) a state-only baseline. Do NOT reintroduce it as a Stein/Q-Prop action-dependent CV (mirage). Benchmark: main-head EV median and volatility vs Stage-1 baseline; policy performance (val acc net of params) must not regress. Threshold to keep: EV volatility down materially with no policy-quality loss.

**Stage 3 — Fix escrow telescoping (only if you keep escrow; ~2 days).** Remove the delta-clip from the reward path; instead feed the unrealized-credit/lifecycle-stage state into the observation and let the recurrent critic predict the delta. Verify Ng-invariance with your existing telescoping test. If you need time/history dependence, adopt Devlin & Kudenko (2012) dynamic potentials or Forbes et al. (2024) episodic-boundary form.

**Stage 4 — Optional: prototype a group-relative baseline (~1 week).** If paired-host/paired-seed rollouts are affordable, test an RLOO-style leave-one-out baseline (unbiased; avoid GRPO std-normalization per Dr. GRPO) as an alternative that sidesteps the Q-critic entirely. Keep it as a parallel experiment, not a replacement, because it weakens per-step credit.

**What would change these recommendations:** If Stage-1 marginalization alone lifts EV median above ~0.5 and kills the volatility, Stage 2 becomes optional polish. If de-shaping degrades exploration or policy quality (watch for the policy ignoring early structural moves whose credit now only arrives terminally), restore the counterfactual term as an auxiliary head with a larger weight, or use Harutyunyan et al. (2015) DPBA to fold it back as a learned potential. If group-relative variance beats your learned V(s) at feasible batch sizes, migrate the baseline.

## Caveats
- **Theory vs. empirics.** Ng (1999), Devlin & Kudenko (2012), Harutyunyan (2015), Forbes (2024), and the Expected-Sarsa / Mean-Actor-Critic variance claims are theory with limited-domain empirics (grid/control/Atari). HRA, PopArt, R2D2, Andrychowicz (2021), RLOO/GRPO/Dr. GRPO carry strong empirics (Atari/DMLab/LLM-RLHF) but NONE on morphogenetic/dynamic-architecture RL — transfer is by analogy. CCA/HCA/COCOA are validated on illustrative + medium tasks, not on your domain.
- **De-shaping can silently move the optimum if Φ isn't admissible.** Only static state-(or properly-bounded history/time-) potentials are guaranteed invariant. Your continuous accuracy potential and any *learned* potential need the Devlin/Forbes conditions; the escrow clip already violates them when it binds.
- **Interaction with causal-masked factored heads.** Marginalizing V over the op head is clean; but your sparse sub-heads are active only ~18–22% of steps, so their advantage statistics are thin — global standardization is dominated by the op head, so consider enabling per-head re-standardization when you de-shape, or the sparse heads' signal may be swamped.
- **Interaction with PopArt-lite.** Because your normalizer rescales targets but not the value head's final layer, changing the regressand (Stage 2) shifts the target distribution the normalizer tracks; re-warm the running stats after the switch, and consider full PopArt if the value scale drifts as the policy improves.
- **Group-relative baselines give trajectory-level, not per-step, credit** — adopting them trades away the very per-step counterfactual signal you are trying to preserve. Keep them as an alternative, not the primary path.
- **COCOA caution:** measuring counterfactual contribution w.r.t. states (not rewards) collapses to high-variance REINFORCE — make sure your leave-one-out is reward-referenced (it is) to stay on the low-variance side.
